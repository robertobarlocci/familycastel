How it works

Four moving parts. That is the whole game.

  1. 🗡️

    A Sidequest appears

    You post something that needs doing. One-time, daily, weekly or repeating. Open to whoever grabs it first, or assigned to one child. The kingdom is never peaceful for long.

  2. 🪙

    They do it, you approve it

    The child marks it done and you get one tap to approve, adjust the reward, or say not yet. Coins land in their purse. XP lands forever.

  3. ✨

    Their world grows

    XP raises their level, and their level visibly upgrades their world: camp, village, castle, kingdom, epic kingdom. Football fans get street pitch to championship arena instead.

  4. 🎁

    Coins buy real things

    Screen time, choosing the film, a big saving goal like a Nintendo Switch. They ask, you approve, the Coins are reserved so they cannot be spent twice.

Why "Castel" and not "Castle"?

Because that is how we say it.

Say it out loud the Spanish way — cas-TEL — with the weight on the second half. It started as one missing letter. Then everybody in the family kept saying it on purpose, because it sounded better, and after a while nobody was joking any more.

A castle is a building. A Castel is ours.

There is also a coincidence we cannot take any credit for. In Catalonia, a castell is not a building at all — it is a human tower, a whole crowd of people standing on each other's shoulders, with the smallest, lightest child climbing all the way to the top while everyone below holds steady. Nobody planned that. It is still the best possible picture of what this thing is for.

For the kids

If it looks like admin software, the design is wrong. That is the rule.

  • Sign in without an email address. A secret number, or a QR code you print and stick on the fridge.
  • Two worlds. A fantasy kingdom or a football world, each with five stages that unlock as they level up.
  • Celebrations that go off. Confetti, sounds, achievements with rarities from Common to Legendary.
  • A Journal that never forgets. Everything they ever earned, spent, asked for and achieved.
  • It installs on the tablet. Add it to the home screen and it opens like an app.

For the parents

The part you actually use is a list of buttons and an approvals queue.

  • An economy you can trust. Coins and XP only ever move through an append-only ledger. Balances are calculated, never edited. Double-tapping a button cannot award twice.
  • Nothing is ever deleted. Archive a quest, a reward or a whole child — the history stays complete.
  • Approve, adjust or decline. Change the reward at approval time when the job was half done.
  • Kids can pitch. They suggest things they did, and wish for rewards and big saving goals. You decide.
  • Backups and updates in the browser. One button backs up, verifies, installs and rolls back by itself if anything fails.
  • Stay signed in. Your phone stays logged in, but wiping or downloading the family's data always asks for your password again.

Put it on your own web space

It was built for the cheap shared hosting you probably already have. Upload, open, install.

  1. Download the release ZIP and unpack it into your web space, or any folder inside it.
  2. Create an empty MySQL or MariaDB database in your hosting panel.
  3. Open the address in a browser. The installer starts by itself and checks your server.
  4. Prove the space is yours by reading a short code from a file over FTP or the file manager.
  5. Pick a password, add your children, and print their QR codes.

What your host needs

PHP
8.2 or newer
Database
MySQL 5.7+ or MariaDB 10.4+
Web server
Apache with .htaccess support
Also nice
HTTPS, which every host gives you free now

No shell access. No Composer, no Node, no cron jobs, nothing running in the background. If your host ignores .htaccess files, the installer tells you to stop rather than leaving your family's data readable.

Questions families ask

Why is it called Family Castel and not Castle?

Because that is how our family says it. Say it the Spanish way, cas-TEL, with the weight on the second half. It started as one missing letter, everybody kept saying it on purpose, and it stuck. A castle is a building. A Castel is ours.

Is it really free?

Yes. It is MIT licensed and there is nothing to buy, no account to make and no subscription. You need somewhere to put it, which for most people is web hosting they already pay for.

Where does our family data live?

On your own web space, in your own database. There is no company server in the middle, no analytics, no advertising and nothing shared with anyone. One installation is one family.

What do I need to run it?

Apache with .htaccess support, PHP 8.2 or newer, and a MySQL or MariaDB database. No shell access, no Composer, no Node, no cron jobs. Ordinary shared hosting is exactly what it was built for.

Do the children need their own phone or email address?

No. A child signs in with a four to six digit secret number, or by scanning a QR code you can print and stick on the fridge. There are no email addresses and no accounts anywhere else.

Can a child lose progress?

XP never goes down and history is never deleted. Coins are spent on rewards, so those go up and down, but every single change stays in the Journal forever. Even archiving a child keeps their whole story.

Is it finished?

It is early days and honest about it. Everything described on this page works today, but the version number starts with a zero for a reason, and it is a family project rather than a product with a support desk.

Go and build a kingdom

It is one download and about fifteen minutes. The hard part is deciding what a clean room is worth.
